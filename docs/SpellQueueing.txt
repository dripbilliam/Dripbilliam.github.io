Players can queue up a delimited list of spells they want to cast on themselves. You type `-sq (spell IDs)` to queue up your spells.

- You can use `,` or `|` to separate spell IDs for your spell queue

Example:

`-sq 78x|639`

`-sq 78x,639`

Both of the above cast Extended Haste then Epic Mage Armor


-sq 33|33|33|33|33|33|33|33|33|33

Cast “Cure Minor Wounds” 10x in a row.


-sq 49|9|13|355

Cast “Bear’s Endurance,” “Bull’s Strength,” “Cat’s Grace,” and “Owl’s Wisdom”


- Metamagic is supported using this list of suffixes:

EXTEND   = "x"
EMPOWER  = "e"
MAXIMIZE = "m"
SILENT   = "s"
STILL    = "l"
QUICKEN  = "q"
- To help you build your spell queue, you can type `-sq debug` to toggle on showing the caster level and spell ID of spells when you cast them. And how spell queue is reading your queue.

- I recommend creating a Custom Text Macro for each set of spells you want to cast. Long buffs, short buffs, etc.

Spellqueue: Storage
- Store up to five spell queues that you can execute using the slot name:

`-sq save <slot> <spell IDs>`

`-sq <slot>`

Example:

`-sq save a 78x,113`

`-sq a`

The above saves spells into slot 'a', then `-sq a` queues whatever is saved there. You can overwrite what's saved in a slot.


Note: You must save using slot names `a`, `b`, `c`, `d`, or `e`.

- To view your stored spell queue slots:

`-sq display`

Spellqueue: Target Mode
- You can add a `t` to spell queue commands to cast *beneficial* spells on a target. Your cursor will change allowing you to click your target.

- Self casting still works just don't include the `t`.

Example:

`-sq t <spell IDs>`

`-sq t 78x,113`


`-sq t <spell slot>`

`-sq t a`
